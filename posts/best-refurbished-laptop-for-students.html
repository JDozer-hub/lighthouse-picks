What to skip, and why

8 GB RAM in a machine you expect to last three years

It is the first thing to become a bottleneck, and on many thin laptops it is soldered and unupgradable. 16 GB is the difference between a laptop that lasts the course and one you replace halfway.

Any refurb without a stated battery health figure

A worn battery is the hidden cost that wipes out the saving. Ask for the cycle count or a battery health percentage; if the seller will not provide it, treat the battery as needing replacement.

Buying the cheapest listing from an unknown seller

On ex-business machines, the difference between listings is usually whether the keyboard, screen and hinges have been checked. Seller rating and a stated returns policy matter more than a few pounds off.

How to decide in 60 seconds

  • Insist on 16 GB RAM unless it is explicitly user-upgradable.
  • Ask for battery cycle count or health before buying.
  • Check the ports you need: many modern ultraportables dropped HDMI and USB-A.
  • Prefer ex-business chassis — they are built for daily handling and parts are easier to find.

Set a budget for a replacement battery in year two. Even a good refurb will likely need one before the course ends, and budgeting for it turns a risk into a plan.
