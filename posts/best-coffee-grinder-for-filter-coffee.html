For filter coffee the grinder matters more than the brewer. An even grind makes a better cup from a cheap dripper; an uneven grind makes a bad cup from an expensive one.

The trap is the blade grinder: it chops rather than grinds, producing a mix of dust and boulders in the same batch.

What to skip, and why

A blade grinder, at any price

It chops inconsistently, so some grounds over-extract and turn bitter while others under-extract and taste sour — in the same cup. No brewing skill fixes that.

A grinder with an espresso-fine setting only

If it cannot go coarse enough for filter, it is the wrong tool. Check the adjustment range covers filter grind, not just the finest setting.

Believing a burr grinder never needs cleaning

Oils from roasted coffee build up on the burrs and stale the next batch. A brush and an occasional purge is part of owning one.

How to decide in 60 seconds

  • One or two cups a day, budget-conscious? Hand conical burr.
  • Grinding every morning without effort? Electric stepped burr.
  • Brewing espresso too? You need stepless adjustment — stepped will frustrate you.
  • Filter grind is medium-coarse. If a grinder cannot reach coarse, it is not for this job.

Grind setting is the variable worth learning first, before buying a new brewer. Most 'wrong' filter coffee is a grind problem, not an equipment problem.
