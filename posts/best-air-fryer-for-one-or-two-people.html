Air fryers are sold by capacity, and the market pushes you toward big ones. For one or two people a large model wastes counter space, takes longer to preheat and is harder to clean.

The real decision is drawer shape, not litres: a wide shallow basket cooks a single portion better than a deep narrow one.

What to skip, and why

A 6-8 litre family model for one or two people

It takes up most of a small counter, takes longer to come to temperature, and a single portion rattles around in a basket that is far too big to cook evenly. Larger is not better here.

Paying extra for a large digital screen and preset programs

Nearly every recipe reduces to a temperature and a time. Presets you will never press are the most common way to pay $60 more for the same cooking.

Air fryers advertised at very high wattage as a quality signal

High wattage mainly means a bigger element for a bigger basket. In a small model it can mean the food browns before it cooks through.

How to decide in 60 seconds

  • Cooking for one, small kitchen? 2-3 litre single drawer.
  • Cooking for two, or batch-cooking? 3-4 litre square basket.
  • Cooking two dishes that need different times? Dual basket — otherwise not worth the counter space.
  • Check the basket against your counter depth before buying, not after.

Baskets, not litres, determine how well a single portion cooks. Measure your counter first — an air fryer that does not fit gets used twice and then lives in a cupboard.
