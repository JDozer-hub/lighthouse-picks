What to skip, and why

A gaming monitor for desk work

High refresh rate and low response time make no difference to documents, and the money comes out of panel quality and stand adjustment — the two things that do matter here.

A curved panel under 30 inches

The curve only helps at large widths where the edges would otherwise be far from your eye. On a 24 or 27-inch screen it usually just makes straight lines look slightly bowed.

A monitor with a fixed tilt-only stand

Almost every complaint about neck strain traces back to a screen that cannot be raised. If the stand is not height-adjustable, budget for a VESA arm as well.

How to decide in 60 seconds

  • Measure your eye height when seated, then check the stand's height range reaches it.
  • Text-heavy work? Prefer higher resolution and IPS, not refresh rate.
  • Two windows side by side? 27-inch 1440p or an ultrawide.
  • Stand only tilts? Add a VESA arm to the budget before you buy.

A second-hand business monitor is often the best value in this category — offices sell off height-adjustable IPS panels constantly, and they usually have better stands than new budget models.
